THE FRAGRANCE GUIDE

A little know-how goes a long way.

From the first spray to the last drop: practical answers for keeping your favourite fragrances at their best.

Where should I spray perfume?

Spray lightly on clean, dry skin, such as your wrists, neck or inner elbows. Start with one or two sprays, then adjust to the fragrance and setting. Let the scent settle naturally.

Should I rub my wrists after spraying?

No need. Let the fragrance air-dry instead of rubbing your wrists together. This is a simple way to enjoy how the notes develop on your skin.

Do I need to let a new perfume macerate?

Usually not. Maceration is generally a controlled step during perfume production, not something every finished bottle needs at home. Resting a bottle is optional and does not guarantee stronger projection or longer wear.

What is the best way to store perfume?

Keep the bottle tightly closed in a cool, dry and dark place, such as a drawer or wardrobe. Avoid direct sunlight, hot cars, bathroom humidity and frequent temperature changes.

Can I spray perfume onto my clothes?

You can, but test an inconspicuous area first. Some formulas can stain fabrics, so avoid spraying delicate materials such as silk or leather. Follow the garment care instructions.

How can I make my fragrance last longer?

Apply an unscented moisturiser first, allow it to absorb, then spray your usual pulse points. Wear time varies with the formula, your skin and the weather, so reapply sparingly if needed.

What is the difference between EDT, EDP and Extrait?

These names generally indicate different fragrance concentrations. An Eau de Parfum or Extrait is not automatically better or longer-lasting than every Eau de Toilette; the full formula and how it wears on your skin matter too.
